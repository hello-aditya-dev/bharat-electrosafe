# Product photography — what to shoot next

Every image now on the site is genuine and correctly assigned. This is the list
of shots that do not exist in the client archive and that would materially
improve the product pages.

Nothing here blocks the current implementation. Each page has a complete,
truthful gallery today.

Priority order. Items 1 and 2 are worth doing before anything else.

---

## 1. Bi-Color Insulating Mats — the whole family

**Priority: highest. This is the only product family with no photography at all.**

Missing image: any photograph of a bi-colour mat.

The four images on that page today are the manufacturer's own illustrations. The
only genuine footage in the archive is five product videos (`Bi-Colour1–4.mp4`,
`Bi-Colour Video1.mp4`), and their maximum frame is 1024×576 with motion blur —
too low-resolution to use as a still. One of those videos does show an orange
mat over a grey coin-pattern base layer on the production line, which suggests
the product is being made and could be photographed.

Photography direction:
- Landscape orientation, minimum 2000 px wide
- One complete mat sheet, both colours visible in a single frame
- One close view of a cut edge, so the two bonded layers read clearly —
  this is the shot that explains the product
- One showing wear or a deliberate cut exposing the lower colour, which is the
  stated purpose of the two-layer construction
- Plain, uncluttered background; even lighting, no coloured light that shifts
  either layer's colour
- Both colour combinations offered, if there is more than one

## 2. Bharat Hydro Seal — installed in a concrete joint

Missing image: a water stop cast into, or positioned in, a real construction
joint.

Every current image is the profile photographed on its own. A buyer cannot see
how it sits in the work.

Photography direction:
- Landscape, minimum 2000 px wide
- Water stop positioned in a joint with reinforcement visible around it, before
  the pour — this reads far better than a finished joint where nothing shows
- One frame wide enough to show the joint in its structural context
- One close frame showing the centre bulb sitting on the joint line
- If a site visit is possible, also capture two profiles welded at an
  intersection or a butt joint
- No faces need to be identifiable

## 3. Electrical Insulating Mats — installed in front of switchgear

Missing image: a mat in service in front of a control panel, substation bay or
switchroom.

The page lists control panels, substations, switchrooms, power utilities and
industrial floors as applications, and currently illustrates none of them. This
is the single most persuasive shot the range could have.

Photography direction:
- Landscape, minimum 2000 px wide
- Mat laid flat in front of a live panel or switchgear line, mat occupying the
  lower third to half of the frame
- The equipment must be identifiable as electrical switchgear
- Shoot from standing eye height, looking slightly down the mat run
- Site lighting is fine; avoid flash reflecting off the panel doors
- Get written permission from the site owner before shooting

## 4. Electrical Insulating Mats — dot pattern in a standard colour

Missing image: a dot-pattern mat in black or blue.

Coin, dot and hexa are all listed as available anti-skid surfaces, and coin and
hexa are both shown. The only dot-pattern photographs in the archive are of a
green mat, and the published standard colour is "black and blue" — so those
images were held back rather than imply a colour the specification does not
list.

Photography direction:
- Landscape, minimum 2000 px wide
- One complete sheet plus one macro of the dot embossing
- Plain background, raking light so the raised dots cast a visible shadow

## 5. Electrical Insulating Mats — supplied form

Missing image: the mat as supplied — a 1 m wide roll, 10 m or 20 m.

Dimensions are published as roll goods, and there is no photograph of a roll.
The renders that showed rolls have been removed because they were CGI.

Photography direction:
- Landscape, minimum 2000 px wide
- One full roll standing or lying, ideally with a second roll part-unrolled so
  the surface pattern is visible
- Plain background or clean warehouse setting
- If a size label or batch marking is on the roll, keep it legible

## 6. BharatMembrane — higher-resolution originals

Missing: larger versions of images the client already owns.

Five of the six membrane images are 500×500 at source, which is the largest the
client ever published. They are genuine and correctly assigned, but they are
being displayed above their native resolution on desktop.

Request:
- The original camera files for the existing tunnel and containment photographs
- Minimum 2000 px wide
- Also useful: a membrane roll or folded sheet as supplied, which the range
  currently has no image of at all

## 7. Auto-Glow — installed in a low-light escape route

Missing image: auto-glow mat in service where the low-light behaviour matters.

The daylight and low-light pair on the page is genuine and is good evidence of
the product working. What is missing is that behaviour in a real walkway.

Photography direction:
- Landscape, minimum 2000 px wide
- A matched pair from a fixed tripod position: normal lighting, then lights out,
  same framing, so the two can sit side by side in the gallery
- An electrical room, plant walkway or emergency route
- Do not brighten the low-light frame in post — the point is what it actually
  looks like

## General notes for the shoot

- Shoot landscape wherever possible. Portrait images letterbox in the 4:3
  gallery viewport and crop badly in social cards.
- Fill the frame with the product. The most common weakness in the existing
  archive is a small product in a large empty field.
- Keep backgrounds plain and consistent. A shared background across a family
  makes the gallery read as one catalogue.
- Do not add watermarks, logos or captions to the image itself.
- Supply original camera files, not messaging-app exports. Several usable
  archive photographs have already been through a compression pass.
- Avoid duplicate filenames. The current archive contains several byte-identical
  photographs under different names, which is easy to ship twice by accident.
